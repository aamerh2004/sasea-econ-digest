Daily Economics Brief

Crude oil's break above $100/barrel amid Strait of Hormuz tensions is transmitting directly into regional currencies and markets: Pakistan hiked petrol and diesel prices for a fourth consecutive time and the PSX logged a third straight losing session, while the Philippine peso slipped to P62.535/$1 and the PSEi retreated on inflation fears. Separately, the IMF confirmed its mission will arrive in Islamabad on September 23 for the biannual review of Pakistan's $7 billion Extended Fund Facility, the next formal checkpoint on program compliance.

Pakistan

An IMF mission is due September 23 for the biannual review of the $7 billion EFF. The PSX fell for a third consecutive session (having already retreated below 174,000 earlier in the week) as Hormuz-related oil price gains weighed on sentiment, even as the rupee held roughly steady at 277.35/dollar. The government raised petrol by Rs3.05/liter and diesel by Rs5.37/liter effective September 11 — the fourth straight increase, taking petrol to Rs370.80/liter. On the regulatory side, SECP proposed REIT reforms and regulatory duty on premium imported smartphones was cut by up to 20%.

Bangladesh

Parliament passed the Bank Resolution (Amendment) Bill as authorities continue confronting sector-wide asset quality problems: bankers and industry data cited non-performing loans at a record Tk 6.06 lakh crore, attributed to political patronage and weak default enforcement. Bangladesh Bank disciplined 13 Commerce Bank officials over an alleged Tk 4 crore incentive-scheme irregularity, and Prime Bank joined the central bank's Tk 3,000 crore export-diversification refinance scheme. Separately, former land minister Saifuzzaman Chowdhury faces a bankruptcy petition in London's High Court after the UK's National Crime Agency froze roughly £185 million ($250 million) in his property assets.

Nepal

Reporting continues on the economic fallout from the August 26 glacial-collapse floods: the Trishuli-Bhotekoshi trade corridor was destroyed, with coverage describing national-level economic consequences beyond the immediate disaster zone, and the government is seeking $20 million in international climate compensation. Separate coverage notes reduced USAID funding has constrained flood relief capacity. The disaster's toll stands at over 1,410 dead and 5,651 still missing two weeks on.

Philippines

Foreign direct investment net inflows rose 35% year-on-year in June but fell to a two-month low, and inquirer coverage separately describes first-half FDI momentum as weakening on growth concerns — a mixed signal on investment trends. BofA flagged that Philippine GDP growth could lag Southeast Asian peers this year on weak domestic demand, and BusinessWorld reported job-quality gains stalling on the same weak-investment dynamic. The peso weakened 2.2 centavos to P62.535/dollar and the PSEi slipped as oil prices breached $100/barrel, fueling inflation concerns; Manulife said peso weakness is not yet a structural threat to the bond market. BSP Governor Remolona earned a third consecutive 'A-' grade from Global Finance's central banker report card, and BSP is easing rules to build out an Islamic finance market.

Afghanistan
