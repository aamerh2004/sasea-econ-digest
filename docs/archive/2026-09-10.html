Daily Economics Brief

A crude oil spike above $100/barrel tied to Strait of Hormuz tensions rippled through regional asset prices: Pakistan's stock exchange extended losses for a third session and the government imposed a fourth consecutive fuel-price hike, while the Philippine peso weakened to P62.535/dollar and the PSEi retreated. Separately, the IMF confirmed a mission to Islamabad on September 23 for the biannual review of Pakistan's $7 billion Extended Fund Facility.

Pakistan

The IMF said a mission will visit September 23 for the biannual review of the $7 billion Extended Fund Facility. The PSX fell for a third straight session amid Hormuz-linked oil price gains and political uncertainty, having already retreated below 174,000 on profit-taking earlier in the week; the rupee held nearly flat at 277.35/dollar. The government raised petrol Rs3.05 and diesel Rs5.37 per liter effective September 11 — the fourth consecutive increase, taking petrol to Rs370.80/liter — reflecting global crude pass-through. Islamabad also invited Belt and Road partners into CPEC Phase II, and SECP proposed reforms to expand the REIT sector.

Prime Bank joined Bangladesh Bank's Tk 3,000 crore export-diversification refinance scheme. Separately, London's High Court froze roughly $250 million (£185 million) in UK property belonging to a former land minister under a National Crime Agency bankruptcy petition.
