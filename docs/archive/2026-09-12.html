Daily Economics Brief

Middle East conflict spillover is the dominant regional theme: Houthi seizure of Bab al-Mandab and Saudi pipeline disruptions from Iraqi drone strikes are pushing oil past $100/barrel, hitting currencies and fuel subsidies from Pakistan to the Philippines. Nepal's flood reconstruction bill has been set at $4.78 billion as rescue operations continue, while Pakistan's rupee and reserves show resilience ahead of a September 23 IMF review.

Pakistan

SBP forex reserves rose $1.2bn to $18.33bn in the week to Sept. 4, and remittances climbed 16.5% y/y to $3.66bn in August; the rupee held near 277.3/USD, with Fitch Solutions (BMI) pushing back its depreciation forecast to 2027 and revising end-2026 to Rs278/USD from Rs288. An IMF mission is due September 23 for the biannual review of the $7bn Extended Fund Facility and the Resilience and Sustainability Facility. Offsetting this, weekly inflation (SPI) rose 8.62% y/y on onion and fuel prices, the PSX fell for a fourth straight session before a mild recovery, and petrol/diesel prices were hiked for a fifth consecutive review (petrol to Rs375.82/litre) as regional oil-supply disruptions from the Red Sea and Strait of Hormuz push costs higher. Islamabad also approved a further $200m ADB digitalization loan despite Planning Commission objections, and FBR told a Senate panel it is retaining Rs390bn in refunds under IMF-linked conditions.

Bangladesh

Coverage is dominated by global inflation/oil-price spillover — US CPI held at 3.4% in August amid the Iran conflict, feeding into regional rate and fuel-cost concerns — and a domestic tax administration dispute, with the NBR weighing a reversion to monthly VAT filing from the quarterly system introduced in July amid enforcement difficulties. No new figures on reserves, remittances, or IMF program status appeared in today's material.

Nepal

The foreign ministry set the flood reconstruction bill at $4.78 billion following last month's glacial-collapse disaster, even as rescue efforts continue at a hydropower tunnel collapse site. A related Diplomat piece notes reduced US and international disaster-response funding (including USAID cuts) is straining Nepal's relief capacity, an added fiscal burden as Kathmandu faces the reconstruction cost.

Philippines

The peso hit a fresh record low past 62/USD on persistent inflation fears tied to oil prices above $100/barrel from the Middle East conflict, dragging the PSEi below 6,100. The DOF is preparing to seek a second suspension of excise taxes on kerosene and LPG, and another P5/liter fuel hike is expected September 15. Separately, Washington is advancing the Luzon Economic Corridor as a template for regional infrastructure, newly backed by the EU and Spain; June FDI net inflows rose 35% y/y even as first-half FDI overall lost momentum, and BofA flagged Philippine GDP growth likely lagging Southeast Asian peers this year. The government's planned jumbo bond issue is still expected despite the Treasury reconsidering timing amid volatile conditions.

Maldives

August state revenue reached MVR 2.42 billion, lifted by delayed tax collections. MIRA opened GST registration for foreign tourism providers and clarified the 17% tourism GST's application to resellers and agents, while new regulations target 168 stalled tourism developments to recover $537 million owed to the state and cap construction extensions at 12 months. Malé essential goods prices rose 6.26% y/y in August, driven by food costs, and the central bank (MMA) warned against unlicensed foreign exchange transactions.
