Daily Economics Brief

Middle East escalation is transmitting into the region through oil and risk sentiment, with Pakistan's equity market falling a third straight session on Strait of Hormuz tensions. Donors moved on concessional finance as ADB agreed a $5 billion Asian Development Fund replenishment aimed at the most vulnerable economies in Asia and the Pacific. Nepal's post-flood trade disruption and Bangladesh's record non-performing loans remain the region's two clearest balance-sheet stress points.

Pakistan

An IMF mission arrives September 23 for the biannual review of performance under the $7 billion Extended Fund Facility — the key near-term checkpoint for program financing. Markets are under pressure: the PSX fell for a third consecutive session on Hormuz-driven oil price increases, though the rupee was essentially flat at 277.35 per dollar. On the external-partner front, Islamabad publicly reaffirmed its Belt and Road commitment and invited BRI partners into CPEC Phase II, while the prime minister solicited private-sector input on an export-led growth strategy.

Bangladesh

Non-performing loans have reached a record Tk 6.06 lakh crore, which bank reporting attributes to sustained policy concessions, political patronage, and impunity for wilful defaulters — a systemic asset-quality problem rather than a cyclical one. Parliament passed the Bank Resolution (Amendment) Bill, and the central bank ordered action against 13 Bangladesh Commerce Bank officials over alleged misuse of Tk 4 crore, suggesting a more assertive supervisory posture. Separately, Bangladesh Bank's Tk 3,000 crore Export Diversification Refinance Scheme added Prime Bank as a participating institution, and a proposed rule would give unsolicited bidders a 7 percent edge in competitive bidding for state-owned enterprise revivals.

Nepal

The economic consequences of the August 26 Bhotekoshi floods are broadening from disaster response into a trade and growth problem. The closure of the China-Nepal corridor has pushed traders to reroute Chinese goods south through Birgunj ahead of festival season, effectively deepening dependence on India and complicating Kathmandu's China-India balancing. Tourism and electricity exports were hit simultaneously, LPG supply disruptions triggered price increases and the commerce minister's resignation, and Germany committed €8 million in relief and development assistance. Local commentary is pushing for development planning built around climate risk rather than repeated post-disaster rebuilding.

Philippines

Foreign investment signals are mixed and worth watching: net FDI inflows fell over the first half on growth concerns and market volatility, even as June inflows rose 35 percent year on year to a two-month low. Bank of America projects Philippine GDP growth may trail Southeast Asian peers this year on weak domestic demand, and weak investment is constraining job-quality gains. On currency, Manulife assesses that peso depreciation is not yet a structural threat to the bond market but could become disruptive if it feeds inflation expectations. BSP is easing requirements and offering temporary incentives to develop an Islamic finance market.
